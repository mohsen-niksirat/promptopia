پرامپت فارسی

Please create a separate high-end design poster for each photo I upload, without multi-image collages—output each photo individually. Use an overall 3:4 vertical composition, with the top and bottom sections strictly 1:1 in height, each occupying 50% of the frame. The upper half retains the original photo, preserving the main subject's identity, structure, pose, realistic texture, natural lighting and shadows, and original color atmosphere, with only subtle high-end photographic color grading to give it the feel of an art magazine, independent publication, or exhibition photography. To fit the frame, you may naturally extend the sky, ground, or environmental background, but do not stretch, distort, or alter the subject. The lower half extracts the most identifiable elements from the photo—the subject, outline, pose, and narrative relationships—and transforms them into a complete brand packaging and collateral visual system. Do not simply copy the photo; instead, distill the subject's features into repeatable core graphics,illustration symbols, patterns, color blocks, or identifying elements, then apply them to packaging carriers of different sizes and uses, ensuring all collaterals instantly belong to the same visual family. Automatically select the 5–8 most suitable carriers based on the original image's theme, such as paper bags, packaging boxes, sets, labels, hang tags, tickets, cards, stickers, envelopes, cup packaging, keychains, wrapping paper, or other related items—do not force fixed categories. Use 1–2 main packaging pieces as the visual focal point, with the rest forming a modular combination based on size, horizontal/vertical proportions, and function; organize the layout with clear grids, edge alignment, scale contrasts, spacing rhythms, and large areas of negative space, like a professional brand design studio's packaging identity presentation board, rather than product displays or cluttered mockup collages. Use a soft, translucent, slightly desaturated high-end color palette, primarily in light powder blue, misty blue, sky blue, and airy cool-toned blues, paired with ivory white, cream white, light beige, soft gray-green, and architectural neutrals, with only minimal dusty rose or muted blush as visual pops. Different packaging can vary color areas and combinations, but must adhere to the same color system to create a rich yet unified series feel. Emphasize realistic yet refined paper and packaging craftsmanship, rendering matte paper, textured paper, thick cardstock, translucent paper, embossing, die-cutting, folding, stickers, spot printing, and subtle layering; use soft, natural lighting only to highlight paper thickness, creases, and material differences, avoiding plastic textures, harsh reflections, exaggerated 3D effects, and cheap product rendering. Typography is unified as part of the entire packaging system. Distill a short English main title from the original image's subject name, theme, mood, function, or symbolism, and extend it into minimal subtitles, short phrases, numbers, category names, or micro-descriptions. Text can adapt naturally to different carriers: vertical on hang tags, extending along package edges, wrapping around circular labels, spanning box faces, hidden between graphics, or integrated into packaging closure structures, allowing fonts, graphics, and materials to form a complete brand language together, rather than slapping the same title on every item. The overall presentation is a high-end brand packaging system / visual identity proposal board: rich but not crowded, lively yet orderly, showcasing the full series while revealing the design logic between graphics, text, materials, and carriers. Regardless of whether the original image features people, animals, plants, architecture, objects, food, vehicles, or natural landscapes, build a unique packaging language from the image itself, avoiding fixed product combos, repetitive templates, e-commerce display vibes, and generic brand mockup kits.
